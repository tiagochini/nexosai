# Koin Pix Parcelado / BNPL — NexOS fit assessment

**Research date:** 2026-09-07  
**Scope:** Public information only. NexOS is an Australian company proposing a BRL 3,990 digital/software/marketing service for Brazilian consumers and wants installments (ideally 24x) while receiving funds upfront.

## Executive conclusion

Koin's official product page publicly advertises a Pix-parcelado model of **up to 24 installments** and says the business can receive upfront. Its 24x model is aimed at merchants with ticket médio above BRL 1,000, so a BRL 3,990 offer is directionally within the advertised ticket band. This is **not yet sufficient to select Koin**: public materials do not establish whether Koin onboards an Australian/no-Brazil-entity merchant, accepts this digital/marketing-service category, supports the proposed settlement setup, or contractually takes default/fraud/refund risk. Those are commercial/onboarding and contract questions.

## Key facts (publicly evidenced)

1. **Installments.** Koin's official Pix Parcelado page says “Venda parcelado em até 24x” and separately “Ofereça 3x sem juros ou em até 24x.” For the 24x model, it says interest and number of installments are set according to risk; installments are paid via Pix. [1]
2. **BRL 3,990 ticket fit—screening only.** The same page labels the up-to-24x model “TKM acima de R$1.000.” BRL 3,990 exceeds that stated floor. Koin does not publish a maximum ticket, consumer limit, approval rate, or a commitment that every BRL 3,990 transaction receives 24x. [1]
3. **Merchant settlement.** Koin markets “receba à vista, venda parcelado.” Yet the product-model table says settlement (“repasse”) may be “no fluxo ou antecipado.” The public page therefore supports the availability/marketing of upfront settlement, but not a universal settlement timing, fee, reserve, or eligibility promise. [1][2]
4. **Two advertised product models.** (a) up to 24x: TKM above BRL 1,000; Pix installments; risk-based interest/term; settlement in flow or anticipated. (b) “1+3x sem juros”: TKM BRL 200–500; Pix installments; monthly or fortnightly payments; settlement in flow or anticipated. [1]
5. **Category evidence.** Koin lists Education among sectors for which the solution is made, alongside tourism, pets, fashion/apparel, beauty/perfumery. It does **not** expressly list software, SaaS, marketing services, online courses, intangible/digital delivery, subscriptions, or lead-generation services. Thus education is positive but incomplete category evidence; do not treat it as approval for NexOS's offer. [1]
6. **Refund/operational controls.** Koin says the merchant portal can “estorne, cancele ou informe qualquer ocorrência com o pedido,” and shows receivables/next-30-day forecast. This demonstrates portal functions, not the contractual refund deadline, funded-versus-unfunded refund allocation, customer communication process, or effect on a paid/overdue financed plan. [1]
7. **Fraud/default.** Koin's Pix Parcelado page makes the marketing claim “Zero risco para a operação.” Koin's main page describes a separate anti-fraud offering based on AI, rules, and recovery strategies. Neither fetched public page establishes indemnity, chargeback/default allocation, fraud exclusions, reserve/right of set-off, or Koin's legal-credit-provider role. [1][2]
8. **Merchant onboarding clues, not requirements.** Koin's contact form requests corporate email, company name, website, monthly-sales range (starting BRL 5k–10k), and product interest. It does not publish a CNPJ requirement, Brazilian bank-account requirement, foreign-company acceptance, KYC documents, minimum historical volume, or settlement currency. [1][2]
9. **Integration.** Public pages invite merchants to integrate and mention a merchant portal, but do not publish API docs, checkout model, webhooks, supported platforms, sandbox, authentication, SLAs, or digital-fulfillment/capture rules. [1][2]
10. **Brazilian consumer-credit disclosure baseline.** The CDC treats financial/credit services as consumer services. For consumer credit/installment offers, it requires clear information, including total effective cost, financier, and total payable with/without financing; it prohibits hiding credit burdens/risks and requires responsible credit assessment. This applies to the customer-facing offer/journey and needs allocation with Koin in contract and implementation. [5]

## Claims requiring cross-reference (sales claims / not verified contractual terms)

- “Increase sales by up to 20%,” “ticket médio em até 35%,” instant approval, and “zero risk” are Koin marketing statements—not performance guarantees or verified loss allocation. [1]
- “Receba à vista” must be reconciled with the page's “repasse no fluxo ou antecipado.” Confirm exact settlement date, whether anticipation is automatic/optional, pricing, reserves, clawbacks, and currency/account destination. [1]
- “Up to 24x” is a product maximum, not a guarantee for a BRL 3,990 NexOS buyer. Confirm risk score criteria, rate/CET, down-payment requirement, minimum/maximum order value, and acceptance rate. [1]
- “Education” does not establish eligibility for online course + software + marketing services. Obtain Koin's written MCC/category policy and underwriting approval of NexOS's landing page, terms, refund policy, and fulfilment flow. [1]
- “Estorne/cancele” does not prove refund mechanics or liability after funds settle. Obtain the merchant agreement and operations manual. [1]

## Gaps / decision blockers for NexOS

1. **Foreign merchant and funds:** Does Koin contract with an Australian company? If not, is a Brazilian CNPJ required? Which entity invoices the consumer, needs a Brazilian bank account, receives BRL, pays taxes, and handles FX/remittance?
2. **Prohibited/approved categories:** Written decision for digital software, marketing service, online course/content, immediate access, recurring SaaS, and any claimed outcomes. Obtain the prohibited-category/MCC list.
3. **Ticket/term underwriting:** Max and min order values; whether BRL 3,990 reliably offers 24x; customer eligibility (CPF, age, Brazil residency/bank/Pix account, bureau/risk data); rate/CET and who subsidizes interest.
4. **Settlement:** Contractual payout date, fee, reserve, anticipation mechanics, payout bank/currency, cross-border settlement availability, and reconciliation/reporting.
5. **Default/fraud/refunds:** Who bears consumer non-payment, fraud, cancellation, statutory withdrawal/right-to-regret, disputes, post-settlement reversals and collection costs; triggers and timing of clawbacks.
6. **Integration and launch:** API/SDK/check-out documentation, sandbox, webhooks/status lifecycle, order/fulfilment evidence needed for digital delivery, PCI/LGPD roles, platform plugins, support escalation and SLA.
7. **Legal/commercial documents:** Merchant MSA, product terms, underwriting policy, pricing sheet, data-processing agreement, refund/dispute SOP, and written approval of the precise NexOS sales page before launch.
